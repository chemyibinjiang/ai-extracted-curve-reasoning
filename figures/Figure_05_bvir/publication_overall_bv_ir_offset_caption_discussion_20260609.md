## Manuscript Discussion

After constructing article-linked curves, we used the recovered HER polarization curves as a quantitative substrate for agent-assisted scientific reasoning over curve shapes, same-panel references, source metadata, and catalyst identity (SI Sections 8-10). The agent compared how well each curve could be described by a BV-type model and by BV models augmented with two empirical correction terms: a current-dependent resistance-like term, R_eff|j|, and a current-independent offset, E_offset (Figure 5). The fitted form was

```text
eta(j) = E_offset + a asinh(|j|/j0) + R_eff|j|
```

where BV fixes both R_eff and E_offset to zero, BV+iR allows only R_eff, BV+E_offset allows only E_offset, and BV+iR+E_offset allows both terms. The analysis was restricted to curves with a consistent HER current-sign branch, RHE or overpotential reference, linear current-density axes, at least five fitting points, sufficient fitting span, and |j|max >= 20 mA cm^-2. This produced 3131 eligible curves from the primary HER subset.

Using R^2 >= 0.99 as the high-quality-fit criterion, BV alone fit 1297 of 3131 curves (41.4%). Adding only R_eff increased the success rate to 2448 curves (78.2%), adding only E_offset fit 2241 curves (71.6%), and allowing both terms fit 2603 curves (83.1%). Thus, many published HER LSV traces deviate from the zero-offset BV form in ways that can be summarized by either a current-dependent curvature term, a horizontal/onset-like offset term, or both.

The two empirical terms should be interpreted first as curve-shape descriptors, not as direct measurements of a single physical process. The R_eff term represents a current-dependent polarization contribution. Although this contribution has the same units as an area-normalized resistance, it should not be assigned directly to uncompensated solution resistance. This is supported by two controls: 203 of 368 nominally iR-corrected rescued curves still selected nonzero R_eff, and 162 of these curves had D_R = R_eff|j|max >= 10 mV. In addition, 169 of 371 multi-curve panels showed a same-panel D_R range above 25 mV, which would be difficult to explain by a single shared bulk solution resistance alone. Therefore, R_eff is better treated as an effective current-dependent loss that can absorb solution resistance together with electrode/catalyst nonidealities such as heterogeneous conductivity, porous or composite electrode architecture, evolving active area, bubble coverage, mass transport, local pH gradients, or catalyst/electrolyte interfacial changes.

The E_offset term captures an approximately current-independent shift of the apparent overpotential scale. With the sign convention above, positive E_offset shifts the fitted curve to larger apparent overpotential at a given |j|, whereas negative E_offset corresponds to an apparent earlier-onset shift. This term should not be interpreted as a simple reference-electrode or RHE-conversion correction. A true panel-level reference conversion error would be expected to shift all curves in the same panel similarly, but the same-panel controls showed otherwise: 152 of 371 multi-curve panels had E_offset ranges above 10 mV, 170 panels contained both zero and nonzero E_offset curves, and 25 panels contained both positive and negative E_offset values. The more plausible interpretation is that E_offset captures curve-specific apparent onset shifts. These shifts may arise when different catalysts in the same electrolyte create different local interfacial conditions, including local pH or water/proton activity, H2 activity and bubble coverage near the surface, adsorbed-hydrogen or hydride-like surface reservoirs, and catalyst-dependent low-current kinetics that are not represented by a single BV branch. Residual reference or extraction offsets may contribute in some cases, but they cannot by themselves explain the observed within-panel variation.

After defining the terms in this way, we asked whether the required correction type depended on catalyst identity. We focused on the 1306 curves that failed BV but passed the full BV+iR+E_offset model. To reduce parameter non-identifiability, the agent selected the smallest correction that preserved R^2 >= 0.99 within |E_offset| <= 50 mV and R_eff <= 10 ohm cm^2. This sparse correction was feasible for 1282 curves. Within this set, PM catalysts were mainly corrected by E_offset: 400 of 705 PM curves (56.7%) required only E_offset, 198 (28.1%) required only R_eff, and 107 (15.2%) required both. In contrast, non-PM catalysts were mainly corrected by R_eff: 395 of 577 non-PM curves (68.5%) required only R_eff, 118 (20.5%) required only E_offset, and 64 (11.1%) required both.

The selected parameter distributions showed the same separation. Nonzero R_eff was selected for 79.5% of non-PM curves but only 43.3% of PM curves, whereas nonzero E_offset was selected for 71.9% of PM curves but only 31.5% of non-PM curves. This suggests that PM and non-PM curves deviate from the ideal BV form in different dominant ways. For PM catalysts, the offset-like correction may reflect a catalyst-specific displacement of the low-current/onset region rather than a shared voltage-axis error. Highly active PM surfaces can generate measurable current close to the nominal reversible potential, so small differences in local H+/H2 or H2O/H2 activity, surface hydrogen coverage, Pt/Ru/Pd/Ir adsorption equilibria, or benchmark normalization can appear as a nearly constant shift over the extracted current window. For non-PM catalysts, the correction is more often R_eff-like because the deviation grows with current. This is consistent with resistive or transport bottlenecks distributed through the catalyst/electrode system: finite bulk conductivity of sulfides, phosphides, oxides, hydroxides, carbides, and composites; catalyst-support or catalyst-current-collector contact resistance; ion/electron transfer across the catalyst/electrolyte interface; binder/carbon-network limitations; local pH gradients; and bubble or mass-transport effects. These processes need not be pure solution resistance, but they all create current-dependent losses that a linear R_eff term can partially summarize.

Overall, the updated decomposition changes the interpretation from a single iR-improvement story to a two-term deviation analysis. BV alone captures 41.4% of eligible high-current HER curves, while empirical R_eff and E_offset terms together capture 83.1%. The fitted terms are not direct physical measurements, but sparse selection makes their roles interpretable: PM curves are more often rescued by an offset-like correction, whereas non-PM curves are more often rescued by a current-dependent R_eff correction.
